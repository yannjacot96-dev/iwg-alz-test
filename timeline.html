History of the IWG Criteria

17 years of evolving research — from the revision of NINCDS–ADRDA in 2007 to the clinical-biological construct of 2024.

The IWG has published six major position papers since 2007, progressively refining the diagnostic framework for Alzheimer disease. The central thread: anchoring diagnosis to a clinical-biological construct — resisting the reduction of the disease to its biomarkers alone.
2007
IWG-1 · Founding Paper
Research criteria for the diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease: revising the NINCDS–ADRDA criteria
Lancet Neurology · Vol. 6 · August 2007 · pp. 734–746
Context

The 1984 NINCDS–ADRDA criteria required dementia and post-mortem confirmation. They could no longer account for the unprecedented growth in biomarker science. An international group convened in 2005 to propose a new framework.

Key Contributions
First clinical-biological framework Diagnosis without dementia Hippocampal amnestic syndrome as core criterion Biomarkers as supportive evidence Prodromal AD concept
Scientific Advance

For the first time, AD could be diagnosed in vivo at the prodromal (pre-dementia) stage. The framework required a core clinical criterion — an episodic memory deficit of the hippocampal type (impaired free recall not corrected by cueing) — supported by at least one biomarker: MRI, FDG-PET, CSF, or amyloid PET.

2010
IWG Lexicon
Revising the definition of Alzheimer's disease: a new lexicon
Lancet Neurology · Published online October 11, 2010
Context

The 2007 framework created a conceptual ambiguity: did "Alzheimer's disease" refer to the clinical entity or the underlying neuropathology? A shared vocabulary was needed for both research and clinical communities.

Key Contributions
AD vs Alzheimer's pathology distinguished Asymptomatic at-risk state Presymptomatic AD (monogenic) Typical vs atypical AD Mixed AD defined
Scientific Advance

Alzheimer's disease was redefined as a purely clinical entity — symptomatic individuals with a specific phenotype and in vivo biological evidence. Alzheimer's pathology was separated as the neuropathological process, which may exist without clinical expression. This distinction would become the cornerstone of the 2021 and 2024 IWG position against purely biological diagnosis.

2014
IWG-2 · Major Revision
Advancing research diagnostic criteria for Alzheimer's disease: the IWG-2 criteria
Lancet Neurology · Vol. 13 · June 2014 · pp. 614–629
Context

Eight years of accumulated evidence on biomarkers and clinical phenotyping called for a refined framework. The IWG-2 incorporated new data on CSF, amyloid PET, tau PET, and neuroimaging, and extended the criteria to atypical forms and preclinical states.

Key Contributions
Pathophysiological vs topographical biomarkers FCSRT validated as core test Atypical AD formally included Logopenic aphasia · PCA · Frontal variant Preclinical states refined Mixed AD criteria
Scientific Advance

Biomarkers were reclassified: pathophysiological markers (CSF Aβ42, tau, p-tau; amyloid PET) directly indicate Alzheimer's pathology and anchor the diagnosis; topographical markers (MRI, FDG-PET) reflect downstream neurodegeneration and are better suited to measuring disease progression. The FCSRT was confirmed as the gold-standard memory test, with ~92% specificity for identifying prodromal AD.

2016
Preclinical AD · Comprehensive Review
Preclinical Alzheimer's disease: Definition, natural history, and diagnostic criteria
Alzheimer's & Dementia · Vol. 12 · 2016 · pp. 292–323
Context

Issued from a joint IWG / Alzheimer's Association meeting (Washington DC, July 2015). As biomarker accessibility expanded, a rigorous and unified definition of the preclinical stage had become urgently needed — along with a frank discussion of its ethical implications.

Key Contributions
Preclinical stage rigorously defined Asymptomatic A+ vs presymptomatic Low individual predictive accuracy Ethical consequences addressed Blood biomarkers reviewed
Scientific Advance

The paper demonstrated that the majority of cognitively normal amyloid-positive individuals will not develop symptoms in their lifetime. Binary biomarker thresholds are inherently artificial. Individual risk stratification is not yet clinically actionable. This landmark paper directly anticipated the arguments of the 2021 and 2024 IWG papers against purely biological diagnosis.

2021
IWG 2021 · Clinical Diagnosis
Clinical diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease: recommendations of the International Working Group
Lancet Neurology · Published online April 29, 2021
Context

In 2018, the NIA–AA proposed a purely biological definition of AD based on biomarker status alone, with no clinical requirement. The IWG responded with a detailed critique of this approach and concrete recommendations for clinical practice.

Key Contributions
Against purely biological diagnosis Biomarkers: low individual predictive value Copathology problem highlighted Asymptomatic individuals = at-risk, not diseased Comprehensive comparison table 1984–2021
Scientific Advance

The IWG demonstrated three critical limitations of a purely biological definition: (1) the lifetime risk of AD dementia in an amyloid-positive 65-year-old man is only ~22% — the majority will remain asymptomatic; (2) copathologies (Lewy bodies, TDP-43, vascular) are the rule, not the exception, confounding biomarker interpretation; (3) binary biomarker thresholds are arbitrary and do not map onto disease reality. The diagnosis of AD must remain restricted to symptomatic individuals.

2024
IWG 2024 · Current Framework
Alzheimer Disease as a Clinical-Biological Construct — An International Working Group Recommendation
JAMA Neurology · Vol. 81(12) · Published November 1, 2024 · pp. 1304–1311
Context

The revised AA 2024 criteria further entrenched the biological definition. Societal concerns about labeling cognitively normal individuals with AD, the emergence of blood-based biomarkers, and the approval of disease-modifying therapies made an updated IWG response essential.

Key Contributions
Updated 2024 IWG Lexicon Core 1 biomarkers insufficient alone Presymptomatic AD redefined Vascular analogy endorsed Cancer analogy rejected Blood biomarkers addressed
Scientific Advance

The IWG 2024 paper formalises three mutually exclusive categories: asymptomatic at-risk (amyloid positive, uncertain progression), presymptomatic AD (near-deterministic biomarker profiles: monogenic mutations, Down syndrome, APOE ε4 homozygous with SORL1, amyloid+ tau+ in neocortical regions), and Alzheimer disease (symptomatic, clinical phenotype + pathophysiological biomarkers). Core 1 biomarkers alone are insufficient to diagnose AD in cognitively normal individuals.
